**Author:** Kyla Duran  
**Project Scope:** Two-Week Trial Analysis (1986–2026)  

---

## Project Overview

ORAI’s artificial reefs in Hollywood, Florida are shallow, nearshore structures designed to increase public ocean literacy, facilitate ecological restoration, and serve as a medium for future benthic outplanting efforts (including octocorals and scleractinian corals). 

Because Hollywood sits near the northern geographical limit of scleractinian corals, warming waters and marine heatwaves directly impact coral survival, safe outplanting windows, and site selection for future reefs. Analyzing long-term thermal stress at this site helps inform effective reef design, placement, and management while demonstrating the importance of coastal restoration to local partners.

---

## Data Sources & Methodology

This repository contains historical sea surface temperature (SST) and coral heat stress data for the Hollywood, FL reef area (`26.125°N, -80.075°W`), spanning **1986–2026**:

* **NOAA Coral Reef Watch (CRW):** Daily 5-km Degree Heating Week (`CRW_DHW`), HotSpot (`CRW_HOTSPOT`), and Bleaching Alert Area (`CRW_BAA`) metrics.
* **NOAA Optimum Interpolation Sea Surface Temperature (OISST):** Long-term daily SST records used to evaluate baseline warming trends and marine heatwave (MHW) events.
